# Accounts and storage — what survives a restart

**An account persists across expeditions. A household belongs to exactly one account in one season.** Store live state and deadlines in PostgreSQL. Store the receipt of a command and the facts it caused in the same transaction as its effects. The game can then both resume a half-mined load and explain a long-finished delivery.

## Identity for a small pilot

1. For the private slice, an operator provisions a named account and gives its owner a short-lived, single-use sign-in ticket. The browser submits it in a POST body; exchange and consume it atomically for a server-side session. Reissuing a ticket recovers the **same** account; recovery can revoke existing sessions and keys. No password database or mail service is needed for this pilot.
2. The session cookie is opaque, time-limited, `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite`; use TLS at the proxy. Cookie-authenticated mutations require a session-bound CSRF token and an origin check. Logout/revocation invalidates the server-side session. Generate tickets, sessions and bot secrets from the OS cryptographic RNG and store digests; a game seed is never a credential.
3. Joining an open season creates one household, two settlers and its charter together. A unique `(world, account)` prevents two tabs from founding two holds. A repeat join returns the existing household; it never grants a second roster. The authenticated account is resolved on the server, not accepted from the payload.
4. The owner may mint a named, expiring, revocable bot key scoped to that household/world (`observe`, optionally `command`). Display its secret once. Both a browser and a bot may operate the same household; queue revisions and the same account-level command budget apply to both. A public spectator needs neither a command key nor access to household views.
5. For open registration later, attach a maintained OIDC login integration mapping verified `(issuer, subject)` to the stable account ID. The simulation and household foreign keys need no change. Keep auth material out of game records and exports.

## Relational map (proposed, not a migration)

Use a dedicated database or three application-owned PostgreSQL schemas in one database/transaction domain. `w`, `h`, `p`, `a` below mean world, household, person and action IDs. World-local references include `w`; a person in the next season can never satisfy an old action's foreign key.

| Schema and tables | Keys, links and purpose |
|---|---|
| `wilds_auth.accounts`, `login_tickets`, `sessions`, `bot_keys` | Stable account PK; unique handle and unique secret digests. Credential → account, expiry/revocation; tickets also have `used_at`. A bot key points to the owner's `(w,h)` membership and scopes. Later `identities` has `UNIQUE(issuer,subject)`. |
| `wilds_sim.seasons`, `packs`, `world_defs` | `seasons(w PK, status, opened_at_utc, cutoff_s, last_applied_s, next_* counters, manifest…)`. Keep exact immutable pack bytes by digest, and the canonical generated baseline by world and digest. Immutable `sites(w,site)` / `edges(w,from,to)` may be indexed from that definition for FK/query use. |
| `wilds_sim.households`, `people`, `skills`, `unlocks`, `buildings` | `households PK(w,h), UNIQUE(w,account)`; `people PK(w,p)` with `(w,h)` FK and unique `(w,h,p)` for ownership checks. Person rows carry location/status and queue revision; skills key `(w,p,skill)`. Unlocks unique `(w,h,tech)` record first cause/time; a building instance records owner, prototype, site and completion. |
| `wilds_sim.stores`, `stock`, `allotments`, `claims` | Typed stores for home/cargo, with `stock PK(w,store,item)`; working allotment key `(w,h,site,item)` over the generated baseline. Track `on_hand` and `held`, with `0 ≤ held ≤ on_hand`. Claims keyed by action and resource account for the held goods, pack space, work slot, project site or bunk. A one-slot work face has at most one live claim per `(w,h,site,slot)`. |
| `wilds_sim.steps`, `due_jobs` | Steps identify owner/person, origin command, ordinal, verb/typed params, state, start/due and locked effects. Unique pending positions and a partial unique running index on `(w,p)`; cap the list under the world writer. `due_jobs PK(w,job_id)` has an index on `(w,due_s,phase,job_id)` and one outstanding completion per running action. Rebuild the in-memory heap from these rows. |
| `wilds_sim.shipments`, `marks_ledger` | A shipment is unique by `(w,source_action)` and records actual goods sent. Earnings/spend rows carry signed amounts, owner and unique `(w,source_action,entry_kind)`. Reserve available marks for a recruit at start; settle at completion. Track net available marks separately from cumulative goods supplied. |
| `wilds_log.commands` | Input journal and receipt: world-local admission sequence, authoritative second, seasonal actor/household, normalized input, outcome, original status/body. Unique `(w,account,idempotency_key)` with digest of API version, route and typed payload. Include joins and system lifecycle inputs with identified sources. No bearer secrets. |
| `wilds_log.events`, `deliveries`, `stream_heads` | `events PK(w,event_seq)` with simulation second, kind, typed/versioned payload and cause (origin action/command or parent event). Addressable delivery rows key `(w,stream,stream_seq)` and point to facts visible in that stream. Household/public streams have their **own** cursors and projected payloads. Hidden activity need not leak through gaps in a shared sequence. |

Dynamic verb parameters and versioned event/receipt payloads fit JSONB; balances, ownership, claims, lifecycle states, FKs and deadlines get typed columns and constraints. Use a bounded signed `BIGINT` domain for times/counters in C **and** SQL; a full unsigned 64-bit world seed can instead be stored as 8 bytes. Allocate simulation order/ID counters in the same transaction as the change; don't sort by wall-clock timestamp or rely on insertion order. A fresh account has no stock until it actually joins a season.

Future features have natural owners: `wilds_sim.parties/party_members` would link one group job to several people, with unique active membership per person. `wilds_sim.defence_plans` would hold versioned household priorities; a warned NPC raid records the plan revision locked at its declared cutoff. Archive that revision with its loss report so a later plan edit cannot rewrite the explanation. Neither extension needs credentials or private data in the rules catalog.

## Write and recovery contract

**Authority:** normalized current rows plus durable due jobs are the restart point. A command/event history is also retained for explanation and verification; rebuilding the whole world from a year's events isn't required for startup.

1. Authenticate and check scope/ownership. Under the world writer, look up `(world, account, key)` before revalidating a revision or rejecting a closed season. Same digest → the recorded receipt; different digest → `409 KEY_REUSED`. A revoked/foreign credential cannot use the lookup to read that receipt.
2. For a new input, settle due timestamps in chronological order to the authoritative admission time. Each completed batch writes its own effects, events, removed/new deadlines and high-water time in one transaction. If a batch must yield, don't admit a command in front of older due work.
3. In the command transaction, lock the season row, assign the input sequence, and apply the **staged** core decision. Write modified rows, claims, jobs, input/receipt, and household/public events and stream counters; commit before acknowledging or publishing to memory. A scoped, well-formed command rejected for stale revision or queue capacity in an open season can retain its negative receipt. Malformed/auth failures, transient failures and new commands against the closed archive don't create simulation history.
4. A completion uses the same transaction boundary: consume a held resource, credit its destination, close its job and emit the causal fact together. If the process dies **before commit**, DB state still contains the old job/stock. If it dies **after commit before reply**, the new state/receipt is already there. If the commit outcome is unknown, discard the staged view, reacquire the writer lock, reload, then query the key or job before proceeding. In particular, never award a second shipment on an HTTP retry.

**On boot:** verify database version and pinned manifest, load baseline, current rows, claims, counters and due jobs; compare held balances with active claims. Advance to now (or the season cutoff) before serving current views/commands. A long outage uses [bounded catch-up](TimeAndCommands.md); it doesn't subtract downtime from a job's promised duration. If a required pack or compatible engine is unavailable, keep that world closed to writes and report the mismatch rather than simulating under different costs.

**For replay:** keep the baseline and initial charter, pinned rules/engine artifact, every external input including joins/closures, its assigned `(second, sequence)`, and the end time to advance to. Re-drive the core, including deterministic deadlines, without rerunning bot code or sampling wall time. Compare a canonical hash of gameplay rows (sorted IDs, integer fields, no auth or operational timestamps) and the ordered semantic events. A client that happened to poll every second should yield the same comparisons as a headless run. A private experiment fork gets a new run identity and never writes to the live world.

## Restart versus new season

| Event | Keep | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Process or host restart | Same world ID, clock origin, people, marks, queues, exact due times and keys | Reconstruct memory; settle elapsed work exactly once. |
| Announced gate closure at second `C` | All completions due through `C`, earned totals, final holdings, manifest and chronicle | At `C` finish due completions, cancel pending and future-running steps, release claims, award no partial XP/marks and freeze the world read-only. A restart after `C` obeys the same cutoff. |
| Found another season | Account, sign-in identity and access to old records | New world ID/seed and pinned pack, fresh charter, skills, stock, buildings and marks. Household-scoped bot keys from the old season cannot operate the new one. |

**Operating it:** numbered transactional migrations with a schema version/checksum table; migration role owns these schemas, runtime role gets the needed reads/writes only. Qualify all table names. Separate database-schema upgrades from gameplay pack upgrades; introduce a new pack in a new season by default. Back up database, manifests and referenced artifacts together. A useful restore drill is to restore midway through the [first expedition](FirstExpedition.md) into a scratch database, advance past the missed deadline, and verify the one shipment, marks balance and feed against the uninterrupted run. If event-feed retention is shortened, keep the replay/archive material and support cursor expiry with a fresh snapshot.
