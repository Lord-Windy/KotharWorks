# Ironwood Wilds — gameplay brief

A gate opens onto a wooded river valley. Behind you is a home world fighting a
war it might lose. Beyond the gate are other expeditions looking for many of the
same things. Your charter is simple: establish a hold, send useful goods home,
and decide who else should come through. Which war, which valley and how the
expedition ends can change with the next server season.

**You direct a household, not an avatar.** The pleasure is in watching its
people become good at different things, commit to plans and live with the
results. Realism should make those results intelligible: iron is heavy, a trail
takes time, timber at the stand is not timber at the gate. Detail earns its
place when it changes a decision or helps explain an outcome.

## A few minutes in the valley

Mara walks to Greybank, works the ironstone for three minutes, and walks the
load back. You queue all three steps in her tab. In Kit's tab you arrange a much
shorter timber run. Kit gets back first. You could send Kit out again, but you
leave Kit ready to build as soon as Mara returns. Nothing quietly assigns Kit
another job. On your next visit the hold ledger says who brought back which
goods, when and why a new design became available.

Now there are two units of ironstone. The home gate will issue two charter marks
for them. The same iron, plus Kit's timber, would fit runners and hardware to a
haul shed, taking ten seconds off each future trip leg at the hold. One
household ships immediately and works towards a third pair of hands. Another
builds first and brings every later load home faster. Neither needs a quest
script. A viewer can point to **the same two units** and explain why the two
holds grew apart.

## What gives a season depth

| System                  | What the household actually chooses                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| People and work         | Each person has a location, a load, individual skills, one running action and a short queue. An empty queue means waiting. Long jobs make it worthwhile to cycle through the household; a queue makes frequent clicks optional.                                                                                                                                           |
| Skills and technology   | Practice belongs to the person: an experienced miner works a face faster. Knowledge belongs to the household: its first full timber delivery unlocks Field Carpentry permanently for the season. Later, an unlocked design can still need a qualified builder, actual materials and a suitable site. Losing a specialist hurts without erasing everyone else's knowledge. |
| Home-world supply       | Shipping physically banked goods earns charter marks. Spend marks and a free bunk to bring someone through the gate. Marks spent on a recruit don't reduce the historical total supplied. Home demand can give a season a direction without prescribing how to play.                                                                                                      |
| The hold                | A lean-to can grow into a workshop, a house and eventually a defensible castle. Early improvements change journey time, beds or storage. Later a household chooses what to shelter and how the hold should respond to a raid while the player is away.                                                                                                                    |
| Going beyond the trails | Later, form a party from available household members and commit them to a PvM expedition. Their ordinary queues can't also run. Success opens materials or sites; expedition members can die permanently. Recruit replacements with supply credit. A proposed last-household relief charter prevents an empty roster from locking an account out of the season.           |
| A raid **on** the hold  | Monsters or other NPC threats may raid stores. The household sets a standing plan, e.g. shelter iron first; the report explains what the hold protected and what was lost. **Hold raids never kill household members.** Other players cannot initiate or direct them. There is no PvP.                                                                                    |
| The season ends         | The gate closes and the chronicle remains. A new land gets a new seed, charter and roster. Accounts and the records of earlier holds persist; the proposed default is that points, equipment and technology start fresh.                                                                                                                                                  |

Once two holds are interesting independently, let them change each other's
opportunities. A later charter might fund a **public crossing** with voluntary
timber contributions. One family delays its own construction; when the crossing
opens, a different family discovers its distant seam is now worth working.
Nobody lost goods or territory to another player. Yet changing the fraction of
civic-minded bots changes the geography that everyone actually uses. That is a
population effect worth watching.

## What is worth showing off

The spectator view should answer “why?” A good entry is *“Kit finished the haul
shed using Mara's first load; Mara's next trip to Greybank took 20 seconds
instead of 30.”* Put the original order, the material transfer and the later
travel modifier behind that line. A chart of marks alone would miss the story. A
season summary can pair two households on the same land, or show what became
possible after one settler learned a skill.

The [first expedition](FirstExpedition.md) plays just the woodland/iron/supply
fork. A later season can test a single new pressure, such as warned raids,
against already-understood households. That gives you both something to publish
and an actual hypothesis to investigate.
