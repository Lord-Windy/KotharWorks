# Ironwood Wilds — Flavour & Setting Bible

*Companion to [IronwoodWildsMechanics.md](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md). This document holds **what the world is and how it sounds**: no numbers, no rules. Where a flavour fact drives a rule, it links to the mechanic. Names are tracked in the [Names Register](#11-names-register); entries marked † are still working names.*

**Version:** V1 (tagged 2026-11-24) · **Status:** early draft, actively being shaped. Review notes for V1: [workingQuestions.md](workingQuestions.md). See [§12 Open Questions](#12-open-questions).

## 0. Canon Levels

Every fact in this bible sits at one of three levels. Writers (and `libs/procgen` grammars) must respect them.

| Level | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stated** | Players can read it plainly in game text. | The Homeland is an archipelago at war. |
| **Rumoured** | Characters believe and say it; it may be wrong. Different settlers believe different things. | "The Stewards are the old lords, still guarding their city." |
| **Hidden** | Designer-only truth. **Never** stated in any game text, journal, rumour, item name or API field. It only shapes behaviour and description. | The Stewards are household servant machines. |

## 1. Tone & Era

- **Era:** roughly the late 1200s – early 1300s, pushed slightly forward. Galleys, cogs, and the very first carracks; crossbows, bows, pole-arms, boarding axes, bucklers; **early cannon** (vase-shaped pots-de-fer throwing bolts and stones) that are new, expensive, unreliable and changing everything.
- **Mood:** cold, wet, grey, and tired. Sleet on slate, silt-grey rivers, tar and pitch, wet hemp, charcoal smoke, salt rime on iron. Mud is the default ground texture. Against that, the beasts are startlingly **beautiful**.
- **Low fantasy, zero magic.** Nothing supernatural exists. The only "impossible" things are the First Folk's city and what is in it, and those are technology the characters cannot recognise (see [§7](#7-the-city--the-stewards)).
- **People, not heroes.** Settlers are contracted labour, debtors, veterans and younger children of minor houses. Nobody is chosen.
- **Nobody builds to last.** Everything in the Wilds is for one year. The Night is coming, and everyone knows the date (see [§4.1](#41-the-day-and-the-night)).
- **Naming register:** plain, earthy compounds such as *Greywater, Hook-Ridge, Ashford, Pell Fen-Born*. **No apostrophes**, no Tolkien vowels, no Latin-sounding grandeur.

## 2. The Homeland

- **Never named.** Always and only *the Homeland*. Characters may name their home island or town; the polity itself has no name in any text.
- **An archipelago.** Hundreds of islands strung across the grey water: narrow straits, tidal races, harbour towns stacked up rock faces, every island living by the sea. Travel, trade and war all happen by ship.
- **A Noble Republic.** Ruled by a council of great merchant-noble families; offices are elected from among them, and the state is run like a trading house. Institutions are referred to generically: *the Council*, *the Arsenal* (the state shipyards), *the factors* (the Council's agents abroad).
- **Out of timber.** A century of war has stripped the islands of every tree worth cutting. The Homeland can still build ships — it has the yards, the shipwrights and the sailors — but it cannot grow them. This is why anyone is in the Wilds at all.
- **Households** are noble or merchant houses (often minor branches, or younger sons and daughters) holding a **charter** from the Council to work a stretch of the Wilds. A charter is a contract: deliver, or lose it.

## 3. The Long War

- **Two Noble Republics, a hundred-plus years of war.** The enemy (the Rival†) is the Homeland's mirror: another archipelago, another council of merchant houses, the same ships, the same contracts, the same debts. Like the Homeland, it is never named.
- **No nationalism.** Nobody fights for a flag or a people. They fight out of **duty** (an oath to a house, a contract signed, a family that has always sailed) or for **money**. Hatred of the enemy is rare and slightly embarrassing; weary professionalism is normal. A settler may have a cousin who rows for the other side; free companies change employer between seasons.
- **Fought at sea.**
  - **Galleys:** oared, fast, manoeuvrable, coastal; built for ramming and boarding; need huge crews.
  - **Round ships — cogs, and now carracks:** sail-driven, high-sided, with fore and aft castles as fighting platforms for crossbowmen and archers, and lately for cannon. Slower and less manoeuvrable than galleys, but they carry more and fight from higher up.
- **Cannon changed the war.** Shot smashes an ordinary oak hull, and the **splinters** kill more crew than the ball does. Ironwood doesn't splinter; it takes shot like a slate wall takes hail. *Whichever Republic builds more ironwood hulls wins the next decade.* That is the stakes of every Chronicle.
- **Marines and sailors are mostly the same people.** Crossbows, short bows and longbows, boarding pikes, short spears, falchions, axes, grapnels and bucklers. Armour that drowns you is worse than no armour, which is why **hide armour that floats** (see [§6](#6-the-beasts)) is worth a fortune.

## 4. The Ironwood Wilds

- **A cold, unfeeling continent across the grey water.** Forest covers most of it, from the shoreline to the glaciers, and ironwood is common in it. Short grey summers, a sleet-bound Dusk, low cloud, high wind off the ice.
- **Ironwood** is slow-growing, grey-barked, resinous and extremely dense. Seasoned, it is close to rot-proof, hard to burn, and **does not splinter**; it dents and holds. It ruins ordinary axes.
- **Better the further in.** The coastal stands are young growth, already cut and re-cut. **Old-growth** ironwood upriver is big enough for galleys and cogs. Only the rare **heart ironwood** giants of the deep interior are tall and straight enough for a carrack's mainmast. *(Mechanics: [ironwood grades](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#73-ironwood-grades).)*
- **Mineral wealth:** bog-iron in the fens, hematite in the crags, slate and good building stone everywhere, and saltpetre crusting the cave walls where the beasts den (for the powder the new cannon need).
- **The rivers are the roads.** Broad, braided, glacier-fed, fast and silt-grey. Their channels **shift week to week**: gravel bars walk downstream, banks collapse, fallen trees (sweepers and snags) block yesterday's channel, and ice clogs them in season. Charts go stale within a week. Most travel is by boat: poled punts, keelboats and river barges. A pilot who can *read the water* is worth more than a good smith.

### 4.1 The Day and the Night
- **Five seasons, then the Night.** The Wilds have **Thaw, Spring, Summer, Autumn and the Dusk**, and together they are called **the Day**. **There is no winter in the Wilds**: the cold comes with the Dusk, and after the Dusk comes the Night. The Homeland, far to the south, keeps the ordinary four seasons, and settlers still call the beasts' thick cold-weather feathers *winter plumage*. *(Mechanics: [the Wilds year](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#41-the-wilds-year-new).)*
- **A charter runs for one Day.** Ships bring the Houses in with the Thaw, and **the last Convoy** takes them home before the Night. Nobody stays.
- **The Night.** This close to the ice, the sun goes and does not come back for months. The wind comes down off the glacier hard enough to strip a roof and cold enough to kill a sleeper before morning. The sea freezes, so no ship can come or go. A few have stayed: a crew that missed the last Convoy, a House sure its hall was stout enough. **None of them has ever been seen again.** The next spring's ships found nothing they could recognise.
- **The Breaking.** At Thaw the ice lets go all at once. Meltwater floods tear down the valleys, rivers jump their beds, and the coast is scoured to gravel. Last year's Holds are gone: a scatter of nails, a hearthstone, a ditch half full of silt. It doesn't matter, because nobody charters the same coast twice. The Council sends each year's Houses to a fresh stretch.
- **The year has a texture:**
  - **Thaw:** high brown water, sweepers in every channel, mud to the knee, beasts still in winter feathers. Tents go up and the first seed goes into the silt.
  - **Spring:** the water dropping, the silt drying pale on the flats, the beasts starting to turn. The first galley keels go down, and every House with a carrack in mind is racing to lay its keel before the season ends.
  - **Summer:** long grey days, glacier melt braiding the rivers, the beasts in moult and the pens being plucked. Expeditions push deep.
  - **Autumn:** rivers falling clear, the best travelling of the year, plumage coming in thick. The Rival grows brazen.
  - **The Dusk:** short days, rivers icing from the edges in, the beasts in full winter plumage, the best hides of the year for anyone who can still find them. The raiders are at their boldest. Everyone is counting days. Walls come down to be rafted to the Landing; slipways work by torchlight; the Convoy rides at anchor, waiting.
- **The Rival keeps the same calendar.** Both Republics arrive with the Thaw and race the same Night home.
- **Rumoured:** *"Where do the beasts go in the Night?"* As the Dusk draws on, the dens empty, the deepest first. *(Mechanics: [the dens empty](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#82-plumage-seasons-replaces-torpor).)* Some say the beasts burrow and sleep; some say they walk north into the ice; some say they go to the City. *"The ones who stayed are in the City still."* (`SUPERSTITIOUS`) *"A wise House has its people home before the Dusk."* (`METICULOUS`) *"They found my uncle's cookpot the next spring. Good pot."* (`WRY`)

### 4.2 Holds: build for the year
- **No castles.** There isn't time to raise one, and the Night would take it anyway. A House arrives with charter-issue tents and whatever its hold carried, raises a timber hall, sinks a stone-lined cellar beneath it, and throws up a palisade.
- **Stone is for hearths, kilns, forges, footings and cellars.** No curtain walls, no keeps.
- **The proudest Holds stand behind an ironwood stockade.** It won't burn, and shot only dents it. But every trunk in the wall is a trunk not going home, and the Night takes the wall too.
- **So in the Dusk, the walls come down.** The trunks are pulled, trimmed and rafted down to the Landing for the last Convoy, just as the raiders are at their boldest. Strip the wall too early and there is nothing between the Hold and the torches; too late, and the trunks are still on the bank when the Convoy sails. *(Mechanics: [build for the year](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#9-household-tech-tree-changed), [the Hold](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#121-rings).)*

### 4.3 Outposts: build for the month
- **The Hold is where a House sleeps; the outposts are where it works.** A felling camp in a grove two days upriver, a weir across a side channel, a farmstead on the silt, a scrape of diggings in the fen. A few tents, a sawpit or a drying frame, a ring of sharpened stakes, and a boat pulled up on the gravel.
- **Silt flats.** The Breaking takes the coast, but upriver it leaves the terraces under a skin of fresh grey silt, and the silt is good. A House that sows barley and oats in the Thaw can reap them before the cold. One sowing, one harvest: there isn't time for a second.
- **Every camp is noticed eventually.** Smoke carries. The ring of an axe carries further. The first visitors are a pair of deserters watching from the treeline; then a Rival boat that goes past slowly; then a night of torches. A wise House works a camp hard and leaves before the torches come. A proud one stays a day too long. *(Mechanics: [outposts](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#125-outposts-new).)*
- **Nobody dies for a woodpile.** Raiders want the timber and the stores, not a fight to the last man. When a camp is overrun its people are driven off, bruised and furious, and walk home along the bank. Behind them the stakes are pulled and the tents are burning.
- **Rumoured:** *"Three days at a camp is honest work. Four is asking for it."* (`METICULOUS`) *"They burned the weir. Took the eels, too. Even the small ones."* (`WRY`)

### 4.4 Food: the biscuit and the table
- **Ship's biscuit.** The charter ship unloads it by the cask: hard, grey, and there is always more of it. Nobody at a Hold goes hungry, and nobody is happy about it either.
- **A good table is worth more than it costs.** Fresh eel, slateback chops, barley bread, turnips, a pot of something with skink eggs in it: a House that eats well works well and is talked about well. The Council hears which Houses kept a good table, and it remembers.
- **Away from the Hold, you eat what you carried.** When the biscuit in a camp or a boat runs out, there is no heroics about it: the people pack up and come home.
- **Rumoured:** *"Biscuit again. I've started naming the weevils."* (`WRY`) *"Hunger is a sin against the house that feeds you."* (`EARNEST`)

## 5. Who Is in the Wilds

| Presence | What they are | How players meet them |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chartered Households** | The players. Each holds a stretch of coast and a charter. | Never directly in conflict (zero PvP); they share the Landing, the Yard, the city map's rumours, and the public Chronicle. |
| **The Arsenal's Yard** | The Homeland's dockyard at the main Landing, run by a Council factor. | Households bring timber, iron, cordage, provisions — and people — to build ships there. |
| **The Rival's expeditions** | The enemy Republic, doing exactly what the Homeland does on the far side of the fens and crags. | Seen through rumours, raids on Holds, contested groves, burnt camps, and a public count of launched hulls. Never playable. |
| **Deserters & free companies** | People who walked off from either side and now answer to nobody. | Raiders, scavengers, occasional sources of rumour. |
| **The Beasts** | The Wilds' feathered, warm-blooded reptiles. | Hunted, captured, bred, plucked, ridden, and run from. |
| **The Stewards** | The pale figures of the First Folk's city. | The wall at the edge of the known world; see [§7](#7-the-city--the-stewards). |

## 6. The Beasts

The animals of the Wilds are **reptiles, but warm-blooded and feathered**. Scaled legs and faces, cold patient eyes, and bodies wrapped in **silky, beautiful feathers**: dense, soft and layered against the cold. **None of them fly**; the feathers exist only for warmth. In a grey land they are the only colour: slate-blue, rust, moss-green, frost-white. Settlers who arrive expecting monsters write home about how lovely they are.

Their **feathered hides** are, frankly, **better than anything the Homeland can make**: warm, waterproof, light, and in the best cases tough as mail. Hunting them pays. Keeping them pays better: a penned flock can be **plucked each moult**, the way the Homeland shears sheep.

| Name | Role | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mossback** | Apex quarry; the prize hide | A monitor-like giant the size of an elephant or bigger, wrapped in **long, shaggy, moss-green feathers** that hang like a curtain of lichen and hide it perfectly among the old trees. Beneath the feathers the hide is studded with bony plates **honeycombed with air**. With its trapped-air down, mossback-plate armour **floats**. Marines will pay almost anything for it. Its bite festers. Killing one takes an expedition; capturing one takes a legend. |
| **Slateback†** | Common hide, meat & down | Cow-sized grazing lizards in herds along the gravel bars, slate-grey plumage over tough hide. Good leather, good down for jerkins and bedding, good meat. The bread-and-butter hunt and the first flock most Holds pen. |
| **Teg** | The dog that isn't a dog | Knee-high pack hunters with a russet crest and a tail-plume they flag to signal each other. Imprinted from the egg, they are fiercely loyal: they track with their forked tongues, guard camps, flush game, and can **taste iron in running water**. Every Hold wants a clutch. |
| **Strider†** | Riding mount | A long-legged, high-walking beast with a ruff of stiff feathers, picking its way through bog and shallows where a horse would founder. Bad-tempered, sure-footed. |
| **Shellback†** | Pack beast & living larder | Huge slow tortoises with feathered necks and limbs. They haul, they barely need feeding, and they live for months in a ship's hold, so ships carry them home as **live provisions**. |
| **Fen-skink†, river-turtles, eels** | Livestock | Egg clutches, meat, down, and fat for lamps and caulking. |

**Why bring them home alive:** the Arsenal wants breeding stock for hides and down it can't otherwise get. Homeland noble houses want the most beautiful of them for their menageries. Ships want shellbacks in their holds.

**Avoid:** flight, fire, venom-spitting, anything draconic or magical. These are animals: large, strange, gorgeous, entirely natural, as far as anyone knows.

**Hidden:** they *were* bred for this place. The First Folk shaped them for this climate, as livestock, as pets, and as the fauna of their parks. They are part of the city's estate, which is why the Stewards ignore them ([§7.3](#73-the-truth-hidden--designers-only)).
**Rumoured:** *"Nothing wild is this beautiful."* *"The feathers are too fine — someone kept these once."*

## 7. The City & the Stewards

### 7.1 What players see (Stated)
- Up the rivers lies **the City**: the one great ruin of the **First Folk**, and the only one on the continent. It is **vast**, far bigger than any charter can reach. Each year's coast faces a different **quarter** of it, whose outskirts begin in the middle reaches and whose heart lies far inland, near the ice. The Council issues a **map** of that quarter's streets on the first day. Every House has a copy, but nobody knows what any of the buildings *are*.
- The stone seems seamless and poured rather than laid. The halls are warm in the Dusk, and lit with no flame anyone can find. Doors have no hinges. Rooms are clean.
- Within it walk the **Stewards**: tall, pale, still-faced figures **made in the likeness of people**. They do not speak to intruders (or speak, politely, in no tongue anyone knows). They do not tire, do not bleed, and **cannot be killed**. Strike one down and it gets up again. Drive a few off and more come back. Stay too long and the whole quarter turns out. **They are why nobody gets very far into the City.**
- Expeditions are sent in with an objective and come back with a story. They might bring a few things out, or never come back at all.
- **The Rival goes in too.** Nobody ever meets them there, and there are never Rival dead to find, because the Stewards tidy up. What scouts find are traces, and only where the Stewards haven't cleaned yet: a chalk mark in a Rival hand, half scrubbed away; a scrap of Rival-cut feathered cloak caught on a railing. Rival names for City buildings drift back through deserters and captured papers and turn up as rumours on the Chronicle. *(Mechanics: [Rival traces](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#114-scouting--map-labels).)*
- **The Stewards ignore the beasts.** A teg can wander a City street unmolested. A clever House eventually notices, and its people start going in **dressed as the local animals**, in feathered cloaks and crested hoods, shuffling along on all fours. This works far better than it has any right to.
- Things carried out of the City (**salvage**) are wondrous to colonists:
  - **Old steel**: vessels and plates of a metal no Homeland smith can match. Pots, pans and lids are hammered flat and riveted into **armour**; a marine wearing a refashioned stewpot as a breastplate is a familiar sight.
  - **Glass-stones**: brilliant coloured gems, shipped home as treasure.
  - **Curios**: objects that are plainly made but do nothing anyone can discover. They are shipped home for the Council's scholars to puzzle over.

### 7.2 What colonists believe (Rumoured)
No belief is ever confirmed. Each settler's temperament leans them toward one (the journal grammar uses it):
- *The Stewards are the First Folk themselves, deathless, guarding their city.* (`SUPERSTITIOUS`)
- *They are soldiers, and the City is a fortress.* (`STOIC`, `GRIM`)
- *They are ghosts, or saints, or demons.* (`SUPERSTITIOUS`, `EARNEST`)
- *They are something the First Folk made, the way we make clocks.* (`METICULOUS`, never more specific than this)
- *Who cares, the pans are good.* (`WRY`)

### 7.3 The truth (Hidden — designers only)
- The First Folk did not die out. **They left.** The City is what they didn't bother to take.
- The Stewards are **ordinary domestic and maintenance robots**: household and municipal staff, built to look like their owners. They are still doing their jobs: cleaning, repairing, tending, and **pest control**, keeping the City ready for owners who are never coming back. To them, humans are **vermin**, and an infestation that keeps coming back gets a bigger response each time.
- The beasts are part of the estate (livestock, pets, park fauna), so pest control leaves them alone. A human in a feathered cloak, moving like an animal, falls under the beast rule. The Stewards are **not very smart**: their rules are simple, and they can be gamed.
- Their "weapons" are **pest-control and maintenance equipment**. They are dangerous because they are strong, tireless, unbreakable, and utterly indifferent.
- All real knowledge lives in **systems built invisibly into the City**. Nobody can see it, so nobody can learn from it. Nothing truly dangerous is ever recovered, because nobody would recognise it or know how to work it.
- **Salvage is junk.** Old steel is kitchenware. Glass-stones are **plastic rhinestones**, and the Homeland pays for them as gems. Curios are appliances, toys and fixtures.
- **The buildings are ordinary modern buildings**: offices, flats, schools, a hospital, shops, a car park, a laundromat, a gym, a transit station.

### 7.4 Reading the City (the map labels)
When a scout reports on a building, the House map gets **a label written by that scout**, generated from what they saw and who they are. A modern reader can usually tell what the building really is from the description. The settlers cannot, and their labels are sincere, confident, and often hilariously wrong.

| What it really is (Hidden) | What the scout saw (Stated) | What goes on the map |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Office block | *Glass cells in rows, each with a chair and a dead black window* | **"The Prison"** (`GRIM`) / **"A Counting-House"** (`METICULOUS`) |
| Multi-storey car park | *Bare floors stacked on pillars, ramps winding up, no rooms at all* | **"The Ramp Fortress"** |
| School | *Small chairs in rows facing one wall; coloured pictures, badly drawn* | **"Barracks for Children"** / **"A Shrine"** |
| Laundromat | *A row of round iron altars, each with a glass eye* | **"The Temple of Round Altars"** |
| Gym | *Benches with weights chained to them; machines for pulling and pressing* | **"The Torture House"** |
| Shopping centre | *A great hall of empty stalls under a glass sky* | **"The Market of the Dead"** |
| Hospital | *Narrow beds on wheels, curtains on rails, everything white* | **"The Hall of the Dying"** (sometimes accidentally right) |

Rules:
1. **Descriptions are honest, labels are guesses.** The description must let an attentive modern reader infer the truth; the label must never use the true word.
2. **Labels differ by scout.** Two scouts, or two Houses, can label the same building differently. Rival labels spread as rumours on the Chronicle.
3. **Sometimes they're right by accident.** Not often.
4. **First to find it names it.** The House that first discovers a building or a place in the Wilds names it for everyone, and that name goes into the Chronicle. So the world fills up with names like *the Ramp Fortress*, *Pell's Drowned Reach* and *the Temple of Round Altars*, and they stick for the whole season. Discovery names follow the naming register in [§1](#1-tone--era).

### 7.5 Rules for writers & grammars
1. **Modern concepts stay out of the text.** The characters don't have them, so the text doesn't either. This is enforced in two parts:
   - **Lint list (hard ban).** Words with no period meaning. They must never appear in any game text, journal, rumour, label, item name or API field, and a grammar test fails the build if one does: *robot, android, automaton, computer, program/programme, plastic, electricity, electric, battery, technology, car, car park, laundromat, gym*, and their equivalents.
   - **Meaning rule.** Words that are fine in period use but must **never be used for City things or the Stewards**: *machine, mechanical, engine, power, office, school, hospital, shop, station*, and any true building archetype. "Offices are elected" and "the Council's power" are fine; "a Steward machine" and "an office block" are not. This one needs a human (or a reviewing agent) to read for it.
   - *Later version (stretch):* grow both parts into a **period dictionary**: modern word → period equivalent (or "no equivalent: describe the effect"), with the canon level each entry may appear at. Not needed for the first release.
2. **Describe effects in period terms**, never causes: *"a sound felt in the teeth rather than heard"*, *"a sweet grey fog that closed the throat"* (fumigation), *"by morning the floor was clean, and Jory was gone"*.
3. **Stewards tidy up.** The fallen are never found again. There are no bodies to recover and nothing to loot from the dead.
4. **Stewards never leave the City.** They evict; they do not pursue beyond its edge, and they never come to the Holds. (Behaviour stated; the reason is hidden.)
5. **Stewards are unhurried, never angry.** Even at their worst they are calm, polite and thorough, like a cleaner who has found mice.
6. **Let the irony be quiet.** A spoon worn as an amulet. A shipment of "rubies" earning a House a commendation. A scholar's letter from the Homeland thanking a House for "the reliquary" (a bread bin). A crossbowman crawling past a Steward in a slateback cloak, and the Steward politely stepping around him. Never wink at the player.

## 8. What the Homeland Needs (Exports)

- **Ships, above all.** A ship is the most complicated thing this age can build. It isn't just a hull: it is ironwood frames and planking, iron fittings and anchors, cordage and sailcloth (or oars and rowing benches), pitch and tallow, stores, perhaps a cannon or two. Above all it is **a crew**: people who must be fed, housed, trained and armed before the ship sails. **Sending a ship home means sending people home.**
- **Ships take time.** A galley wants a season on the slip, a cog two, and a carrack three. A carrack's keel has to be down by the end of Spring or it won't swim before the Night; a carrack laid on the last day of Spring goes down the ways on Night-eve, with the Convoy waiting for her. The Arsenal is famous for working from pre-cut parts, and the Yard does the same.
- **The keel is the commitment.** Nobody counts a ship as begun until her keel is down, and a keel is old-growth timber hauled from the middle reaches. Only a carrack's mainmast must be heart ironwood.
- **The Yard** at the main Landing is where ships are built in common. Any House may bring materials and people. Each launch is entered on the public Chronicle with its muster roll and the Houses that built it. Late in the year the Yard lays down nothing it can't finish.
- **Built alone.** A proud (or reckless) House may try to build an entire ship, crew and all, on its own slipway. A slipway needs a **slipway kit**: a master shipwright's chest of tools, the launching ways, and the Arsenal's warrant to build. The Council only grants these to a House with the standing to deserve them, and the kit fills most of a charter ship's hold. A House saves for years to try it. A slipway holds one hull at a time, so a House that wants a carrack lays its keel the moment the ways are clear. If the ship launches, the House's name is on every dispatch and the hull goes on the House's **Fleet List** for good. Years later a dispatch may still mention her (*"the Grey Heron, built alone by House Ashford, took a Rival cog off the Narrows"*). If it fails, the Council credits the House with nothing.
- **Direct consignments** go home on the scheduled **Convoy**: timber, iron, finished arms, mossback-plate, hides and down, live beasts, poultices and medicines, and salvage. **The last Convoy** sails on the eve of the Night, and everyone goes home with it.
- **Supply Favor** is the Council's ledger of what a House has delivered. It buys fresh settlers off the next ship.
- **The race with the Rival** is a matter of pride. Dispatches compare the season's launches, and nobody is paid for winning.

### 8.1 The House ship
- A House that has built a great merchantman of its own (a **great cog†**) keeps her. She is the **one thing that outlasts the Night**, because she isn't there for it: she sails home before the ice and comes back with the Thaw.
- She anchors off the House's coast as its first hall and strongroom, so her people sleep aboard while the tents go up around everyone else's. Over the years she is fitted out: a shipwright's loft, a forge on deck, pens below, a deeper hold. She is too big for the rivers and never goes up them.
- Her sailors are Homeland hands who come with the ship, not the House's settlers. *(Mechanics: [the House ship](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#107-the-house-ship-stretch-new).)*

## 9. Chronicles (Seasons) in the Fiction

Each **Chronicle** is **one Day of the Wilds**, Thaw to the Night: a year of the Long War, with a fresh charter, a fresh stretch of coast, a fresh quarter of the City, and a fresh state of the war. **Myth Flags** describe that year: how hard the Rival is pushing, what the Homeland is short of, what is stirring in the interior, whether the Night comes early, and what the Dusk brings.

The Night ends every Chronicle, and the Breaking wipes the coast clean. What a House carries forward is its name, heraldry and Hall of Chronicles, its **Fleet List**, its **House ship** if it has one, and its **standing with the Council** (Renown†). A House that delivered well is granted more of the next charter ship's hold: more hands, better tools, a hall frame instead of tents, and in time a slipway kit. The hold is only so big, though, so even the proudest House starts each year with mud on its boots. Its people and goods do not carry forward. *(Mechanics: [Renown & the Outfit](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#43-renown--the-outfit-new).)*

## 12. Open Questions

1. **Is "bred for this place" Hidden or Rumoured?** As written, players only ever *suspect* the First Folk shaped the beasts.
2. *(Resolved 2026-11: yes, as traces only. See [§7.1](#71-what-players-see-stated).)*
3. **Institutions:** are *the Council*, *the Arsenal* and *factors* acceptable generic names, or should the Homeland's institutions also go unnamed?
4. **"Cold, unfeeling":** with the beasts now beautiful, is the contrast (grim land, lovely animals) the intended tone?
5. **Where do the beasts go in the Night? (Hidden truth.)** One option: they winter in the City's warm halls and parks, as part of the estate the Stewards keep. That would tie the Night to the "Stewards ignore beasts" rule. Or they simply burrow, and the City rumour stays a rumour.
6. **Why don't settlers come back?** Everyone sails home each year, so players will ask. Either one-year contracts (paid off, scattered), or a re-hireable "returning hand" with the same persona and journal and skills reset ([mechanics §13](IronwoodWildsMechanics.md#13-open-mechanical-questions)).
7. **Has anyone ever wintered?** Keep it pure rumour, or plant one Stated trace (a cairn, a journal page found at Thaw) as a recurring mystery?
